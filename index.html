The problem

Creators run businesses that can't raise money.

$250Bcreator economy in 2023, heading toward $480B by 2027
4%of creators earn more than $100,000 a year
46%of full-time creators earned under $1,000 in 2022

Goldman Sachs Research, 2023 · Linktree survey via TechCrunch, 2022

Banks don't lend against views. So a creator who wants to make something bigger has three options. Pay for it themselves, give a studio part of the rights, or sell a slice of their channel's income with no end date.

Fans already spend on memberships, merch and crowdfunding, but they get rewards, never a return.

How it works

One project, one raise, a 1.3x cap.

  1. 1
    The creator picks the project and the budget. A film, a game, a product run or a tour.
  2. 2
    CX sets up a small company just for that project, so only the project's numbers are filed.
  3. 3
    Fans invest through an SEC-registered portal. Miss the goal, and everyone gets their money back.
  4. 4
    A payment agent pays fans first from the project's revenue until they reach 1.3x.
  5. 5
    Then the deal ends. The creator keeps the rights and everything after that.

An example: a $100,000 short film, 15% of its revenue

Creators and fans

Both sides know the deal up front.

Creators

Get

  • Money up front without selling the rights
  • Fans with a stake who help launch the project
  • A deal that ends at 1.3x

Give

  • A share of that project's revenue until fans reach 1.3x
  • A short yearly report

Fans

Get

  • A share of a project they care about, up to 1.3x back
  • A raise page that shows revenue and payouts

Risk

  • They can lose everything if the project flops
  • Notes can't be resold for 12 months

The legal path

Every raise runs through an SEC-registered Regulation Crowdfunding portal, which handles investor checks, limits and escrow. A securities lawyer reviews the structure before any real money moves. Notes can't be resold for 12 months, so trading comes later through a registered partner.

What's built

A full play-money exchange. It has an order book, IPO auctions and quarterly earnings filings. It also has a brokerage-style account opening with a risk quiz, and automated tests that click through every trade. Try it.
